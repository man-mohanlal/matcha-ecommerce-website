The 6 Stages of Failda Pure Matcha

Unlike ordinary powdered green tea, authentic ceremonial matcha demands strict agricultural discipline, precise steam arresting, and time-honored slow grinding.

01 25 Days Before Harvest

Tana Canopy Shading

Nearly four weeks before the spring pluck, traditional black reed screens (tana) canopy the tea bushes, filtering out up to 90% of direct sunlight. Denied direct rays, the Camellia sinensis plant pumps intense amounts of chlorophyll and umami-rich L-theanine into its leaves, eliminating astringency and yielding that signature brilliant jade hue.

Sunlight Block 85% – 90%
Key Phytochemical L-Theanine
Wazuka Valley Fields
Ichibancha (First Flush)
02 Early May Plucking

Selective First Flush Hand-Harvest

We exclusively harvest "Ichibancha" — the virgin spring crop containing the highest nutrient density of the entire year. Master pickers examine every branch to snap only the two youngest, tenderest crown shoots and bud, leaving coarser, bitter lower leaves behind.

Harvest Selection Top 2 Leaves Only
Harvest Season May (Once Annually)
03 Within 4 Hours of Harvest

Precise Steam De-Enzyming

To prevent enzymatic fermentation and browning, fresh leaves undergo flash steaming within hours of plucking. For 15 to 20 seconds, gentle pressurized steam halts oxidation, permanently locking in the vibrant emerald tone, fresh grassy aroma, and potent polyphenol antioxidants.

Steam Exposure 15 – 20 Seconds
Oxidation Level 0% Preserved
Kyoto Steam Facility
Pure Tencha Flakes
04 Aero-Separation

Drying & Tencha De-veining

The steamed leaves are cooled with air jets and transported through multi-stage gentle dryers. Next comes the painstaking winnowing process: veins and tough fibrous stems are cleanly separated, retaining strictly the soft leaf flesh known as "Tencha".

Fibrous Veins 100% Removed
Material Form Crisp Tencha
05 Mikage Stone Mill

Granite Stone-Grinding

Industrial high-speed blade mills generate friction heat that scorches the matcha's delicate notes. Failda adheres strictly to authentic granite mills, slowly revolving at room temperature. Each mill takes an entire hour to produce just 35 grams of micron-fine powder (5 to 10 microns), velvety as talc.

Production Speed 35 Grams / Hour
Powder Fineness 5 - 10 Microns
Granite Mikage Mill
Certified Cleanroom
06 Sealed In Origin

Nitrogen Flushing & Lab Verification

Every single batch is independently tested for heavy metals, radiation, and microbiological purity. Immediate hermetic sealing in food-grade aluminum tins with inert nitrogen displacement shields the matcha against ultraviolet radiation and moisture degradation.

Preservation Nitrogen Sealed
Purity Standard JAS & USDA Organic

DAILY PRACTICE

How to Whisk Perfection at Home

Follow our classic master recipe for Usucha (thin tea). Three mindful minutes that completely center your morning focus.

Step 01

Sift The Powder

Measure 2 scoops (approx 2 grams) with your Chashaku and gently press through the Furui mesh directly into your pre-warmed Chawan bowl.

TARGET METRIC 2.0 grams sifted powder
Step 02

Temperature Control

Pour 60 to 70 ml of soft spring water heated strictly to 75°C - 80°C (167°F - 176°F). Boiling water burns delicate amino acids and causes bitterness.

TARGET METRIC 75°C - 80°C / 60ml Water
Step 03

The "W" Motion

Hold the Chasen lightly with your fingers. Whisk quickly from the wrist in an energetic back-and-forth "W" or "M" motion without pressing into the bowl bottom.

TARGET METRIC 20 seconds rapid wrist whisking
Step 04

Surface Polish

Slow down and lightly trace the surface foam in a slow circular swirl to break large air bubbles into uniform jade micro-foam. Sip immediately.

TARGET METRIC Dense creamy jade crema
